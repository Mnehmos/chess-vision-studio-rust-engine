## Method

- Engine: `target-sf3/release/uci.exe`, 1 thread, flags
  `--futility --rfp --tt-prune-store --qtt --histmalus --histlmr --lmp --smarttime --nnue-cal <artifact>`,
  net `target-cvs/matrix-raw.json` + helper `matrix-residual.json`.
- Opponent: local native Stockfish (`stockfish-windows-x86-64-avx2`), `option.UCI_LimitStrength=true`,
  `option.UCI_Elo=<anchor>`.
- 200 games per anchor over **100 distinct positions** (`openings-inv1-20260910.epd`, consumed
  sequentially — no repeats), colour-paired, tc=10+0.1, draw/resign adjudication, 10 concurrent.
- Harness: `benchmarks/scripts/bench_anchor.py` (now resolves artifact paths to absolute —
  cutechess resolves engine args against its own cwd, and a relative path silently kills the
  engine process). Raw PGNs under `benchmarks/results/anchor-nnuecal-20260911/`.

## Note on adjudication across eval scales

This anchor ran with draw/resign adjudication on, and the calibrated engine reports scores on
roughly the true centipawn scale while the uncalibrated one reported them ~2x compressed. Both
engines in a game are affected equally only when they share a scale; across the two anchor runs
the cp-threshold adjudications therefore fire at different real-advantage levels. The draw rate
is nearly unchanged (4.0% -> 4.5% at SF2000), and the direction of the effect is the same as the
no-adjudication gate's, so the +74 Elo reading is not an adjudication artifact — but for
score-scale changes the no-adjudication gate remains the primary instrument.
